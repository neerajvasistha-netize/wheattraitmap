Candidate gene mapping · on RefSeq v2.1, computed live from the current records

All genes, QTL, QTNs and MTAs from every study and disease that fall in the region are combined. Precise positions (genes, QTNs, significant SNPs, QTL peaks) count more than wide QTL intervals, and grade A counts more than D. The core interval is where the evidence concentrates; the rest of the region is trimmed. Defence-related genes (NLR, WAK, receptor kinases, PR proteins, ABC transporters) in the core are listed as candidates. Results change automatically as records are added or corrected.

Choose a region and press “Map candidate genes”, or use “Candidate genes of this region →” in Region search.

Region sequence viewer · Chinese Spring reference

Enter a region on RefSeq v2.1 and the Chinese Spring sequence of exactly that region is shown (up to 100 kb), with copy and save buttons. On the atlas server the sequence comes from the atlas's own copy of the IWGSC RefSeq v2.1 genome. Tip: 0.05 Mb = 50 kb.

Analysis tools · computed inside the atlas on IWGSC RefSeq v2.1

Meta-analysis of all atlas loci directly on the physical map: overlapping QTL, MTAs, QTNs and genes from different studies are merged into consensus meta-QTL with narrower confidence intervals. No genetic-map projection is needed, and the result updates whenever records are added.

What is in the database

Each record is one report of a resistance or susceptibility locus: a cloned gene (CG), susceptibility or effector-sensitivity gene (SG), designated gene (DG), QTL, GWAS marker-trait association (MTA), QTN, bulked-segregant region (BSA) or meta-QTL (MQTL). Positions are stored in megabases exactly as the source reports them, with the reference assembly it used.

Coordinates

Reference frame. The atlas is shown on IWGSC RefSeq v2.1 by default; RefSeq v1.0 (and v1.1, which shares its coordinates) and 13 chromosome-level pangenome assemblies can be selected with “Show positions on”. Every record keeps the position and assembly given in its source paper (column “Published on”). Positions published on RefSeq v1.0/v1.1 (or with no stated version, treated as v1.0) were converted to v2.1 and the cultivars; positions published on v2.0/v2.1 were converted back to v1.0. Merged loci, shared regions and region search are calculated on the assembly being shown.

Conversion. IWGSC v1.1 high-confidence genes within ±5 Mb of a locus are used as anchors: through the IWGSC v1.1 → v2.1 gene mapping for RefSeq v2.1, and by matching gene sequences (minimap2; identity ≥ 95%, coverage ≥ 60%) for the pangenome cultivars. Anchors off the local collinear trend are discarded and each locus end moves with its nearest anchor. A leave-one-out test on 2,000 genes per assembly gave median errors of 0.3–1.2 kb, with 97.5–99.8% of positions within 0.5 Mb. Each conversion carries a quality note.

Curation status. Records are marked as from atlas release 1.2, hand-curated from the paper, or automatically extracted from the paper text (grade B, “auto-extracted … verify against paper”), which should be checked before release. Loci reported only on genetic maps (cM) were placed at the RefSeq v1.0 positions of their linked or flanking SSR markers from Zhao et al. (2019, J Genet 98:69) and of the GWM primers of Röder et al. (1998, Genetics 149:2007–2023), located on RefSeq v1.0 by exact primer matching (128 of 129 markers in both sources agree within 1 Mb); these carry grade C (one marker) or B (flanking markers within 25 Mb) and a note saying so.

Anchoring grades

  • A position given by the original study from sequence-anchored markers or genes
  • B rounded, flanking-marker, approximate or second-hand position
  • C pre-RefSeq pseudomolecule
  • D chromosome arm or deletion bin only (not drawn)

How to contribute

People the owner shares this page with as Contributor or Editor can add and correct records and update the paper list. Every new record needs a source citation; enter only values stated in the paper and note the table or supplementary file they came from. Grade B any position you took from another paper or a review. Only Editors can delete records.

Deployment (RGU server)

The atlas is one self-contained page (index.html) plus one data folder for the genome sequence. Copy both to the web server; no database software is needed for browsing.

Genome sequence for the sequence viewer and primer design. Put the Chinese Spring IWGSC RefSeq v2.1 chromosomes in genome/v2.1/ next to index.html, one file per chromosome named 1A.seq … 7D.seq, each holding only the sequence on a single line (no header, no line breaks). The page then reads any region directly with HTTP range requests (supported by Apache and nginx by default). Prepare the files once with: awk '/^>/{if(f)close(f);c=$1;sub(/^>(Chr|chr)?/,"",c);f="genome/v2.1/"c".seq";next}{printf "%s",$0 > f}' iwgsc_refseqv2.1_assembly.fa (about 14.6 GB in total; the unanchored chrUn is not needed).

Until these files are present the viewer falls back to the public RefSeq v1.0 sequence; inside the private preview neither is reachable, because the preview page may not contact other servers.
